About the Summit

A movement, not a moment.

On February 28, 2026, the inaugural Summit convened in Washington, D.C., bringing together more than 190 registered Black male educators, leaders, and allies for a historic day of professional development, fellowship, and collective strategy.

The response was extraordinary: over 310 educators registered or joined the waitlist, representing more than 20 states and spanning every level of the education system.

The Summit was not simply a professional development event. It was a declaration that Black male educators are not ancillary to the American education project, but essential to it. A movement, not a moment.

What we heard

The weight they carry. The gifts they bring.

The challenges they carry

  • Isolation and underrepresentationBeing the only, or one of very few, Black male educators in a building, district or leadership team.
  • Burnout and disproportionate laborExpected to serve as teacher, mentor, disciplinarian, counselor, cultural translator and role model at once.
  • Navigating white spacesTone-policed, expertise questioned, reduced to behavior-management roles.
  • Lack of mentorship and pipelineNo structured pathways for Black male leadership development and succession.
  • A chilling climateA political environment that discourages equity-centered leadership and programming.

What they bring

  • Authentic relationship-building and trust
  • Culturally responsive, equity-centered leadership
  • Systems-level strategic thinking and innovation
  • Deep content expertise and instructional knowledge
  • Empathy, emotional intelligence and servant leadership
  • Resilience and the ability to thrive under pressure
  • Community building and mentorship
“I bring a justice-centered, relationship-driven leadership approach rooted in integrity, cultural humility, and an unwavering commitment to protecting the dignity and potential of young people.”Jamial Black, Executive Director
